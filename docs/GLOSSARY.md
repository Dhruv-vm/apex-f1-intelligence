# APEX — F1 Intelligence: Terminology Glossary

## 1. Statistical & Machine Learning Terminology

### Forecast Checkpoint
A pre-defined discrete moment in time (e.g., Post-FP1, Post-Qualifying, Lap 15) at which a prediction snapshot is computed and published.

### Information Cutoff
The absolute temporal threshold ($T_{cutoff}$) past which no data, metadata, or event outcomes may be ingested or utilized by a feature extraction pipeline or model. Enforcing strict cutoffs prevents temporal leakage.

### Temporal Leakage (Lookahead Bias)
An experimental flaw wherein features constructed at time $T$ incorporate information that was only knowable or finalized at time $T + \Delta$ (for example, using post-qualifying grid penalties in a model evaluated at the end of FP2, or using the final safety car count to predict tire degradation).

### Probabilistic Forecast
A prediction that outputs a full probability distribution over possible future outcomes (e.g., $P(\text{Verstappen wins}) = 0.38$, $P(\text{Norris wins}) = 0.24$) rather than a deterministic single-point estimate.

### Calibration
The statistical alignment between predicted probabilities and empirical event frequencies. A model is well-calibrated if, among all instances where an event is assigned a probability of $p=0.70$, the event actually occurs approximately 70% of the time.

### Brier Score
A strictly proper scoring rule measuring the accuracy of probabilistic forecasts:
$$\text{BS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K (f_{ik} - o_{ik})^2$$
where $f_{ik}$ is the forecasted probability for outcome $k$, and $o_{ik} \in \{0, 1\}$ is the actual binary outcome indicator. Lower Brier scores indicate superior calibration and sharpness.

### Multi-Class Log Loss (Cross-Entropy Loss)
A strictly proper scoring rule penalizing confident incorrect predictions exponentially:
$$\text{LogLoss} = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K o_{ik} \ln(f_{ik})$$

### Rolling-Origin Evaluation (Walk-Forward Validation)
A backtesting methodology for chronological data where models are trained exclusively on seasons/rounds occurring prior to round $R$, tested on round $R$, and then iteratively expanded to include round $R$ before testing round $R+1$.

### Ablation Study
A systematic experiment where specific feature groups, modalities (e.g., removing weather feeds, removing FP long-run degradation), or architectural components are intentionally removed to isolate and measure their marginal contribution to predictive skill.

## 2. Operational & Platform Terminology

### Ground Truth (Observed)
The immutable, finalized historical record of sporting results, official timing sheets, and sensor telemetry published by the governing body (FIA) and timing providers.

### Historical Replay
The deterministic, time-stepped simulation of a past session or race, replaying data up to a specified historical point $t$ to evaluate models or drive user interfaces exactly as they would have appeared in real time.

### Model Forecast
The frozen probabilistic prediction output generated by a certified model checkpoint at an information cutoff.

### Scenario Simulation (Counterfactual)
A synthetic simulation of hypothetical racing dynamics ("what-if" modeling) departing from actual history, such as simulating race outcome distributions if a Safety Car had occurred on Lap 22 instead of Lap 38.

## 3. Formula 1 Domain Terminology

### Stint
A continuous sequence of laps completed by a driver between leaving the pit lane (or starting the race) and returning to the pit lane for service, run on a single set of tires.

### Pit Loss Delta
The net time penalty incurred by entering the pit lane, driving through the speed limiter zone, executing a stationary pit stop, and rejoining the track, compared to staying at racing speed on track (typically between 18 and 28 seconds depending on circuit layout).

### Undercut
A tactical pit stop executed 1–3 laps earlier than a competing rival to capitalize on fresh tire grip, allowing the driver to produce faster out-laps and gain track position once the rival stops.

### Overcut
A tactical pit stop executed later than a competing rival, utilized when tire degradation is minimal and the driver in clean air can lap faster than a rival trapped in dirty air or warming up hard compounds.

### Tire Degradation Slope
The estimated rate of lap time deterioration per lap (typically measured in seconds per lap, e.g., $+0.08\text{ s/lap}$) attributable to thermal degradation and tire rubber wear.

### Delta to Pole
The normalized percentage or second deficit between a driver's fastest qualifying lap and the pole position lap time set during the same session.

### Track Evolution
The progressive improvement in circuit grip and lap times throughout a weekend as rubber is deposited onto the racing line by circulating cars.

### VSC / SC / Red Flag
- **Virtual Safety Car (VSC):** Neutralization requiring all drivers to adhere to a reduced delta lap time; pit stop time loss is significantly reduced.
- **Safety Car (SC):** Physical safety car deployed on track bunching the field; pit stop time loss delta is minimized.
- **Red Flag:** Full session suspension; cars return to pit lane; free tire changes permitted under current FIA sporting regulations.
